## I. The Fall of Lucifer, and the Life of Adam and Eve

### folios 004v–006v

The book does not begin with a gospel. It begins before the world, and it
begins by telling you who is being told.

In the beginning there is the Lord God, and «heart one on sky and earth», and
«sun and moon write» (004v). Then a voice enters that will not leave the book
for four hundred leaves: «Elijah prophet say angel God» (004v). Everything
that follows is addressed to Elijah, delivered in numbered sayings, and the
numbers run on across the pages — «and six say God angel to Elijah prophet»
(001r), «and seven say God angel to Elijah prophet» (001r). Whatever this
manuscript is, it presents itself as a revelation given to one man, in order.

What the angel tells him first is the fall. The angels are made before Adam,
«many angel on angel» (004v), and the count of years is given: «two half
hundred year seven», two hundred and fifty-seven (004v). One of them has a
name the script writes as a single sign — the sign glossed «hide oneself»
joined to the sign for «angel» (004v) — and that angel «pray», «ten ten ten
ten day, ten ten ten ten night» (004v), the codex's way of writing forty. He
is «[proud]» (004r). He «[envied]» (002r). He «[set]» himself «on throne»,
«[equal]» to the Lord (002r).

Between those two pages stands one of the strangest leaves in the codex. Folio
004r says almost nothing but one thing, over and over, in slightly different
order each time: an angel, and the Lord «on throne», and «cup to hide
oneself», and «cup hide oneself angel [steal] cup to hide oneself above, and
two go» (004r). The page circles. It is not clear what the cup is, and this
edition does not pretend to know; the lines are printed at the back exactly as
they stand, and they are worth looking at, because a page that repeats itself
twelve times is either liturgy or damage, and it does not look like damage.

Then the episode that identifies the whole book. Michael is named — «Michael
angel believe servant stand up up» (002r). The command goes out «from father
God eternal», and the order is that «each, every exist angel» shall «bow
down» (002r); and the hiding angel «[refused] to go» (002r). That scene is not
in Genesis. It is the distinguishing episode of the apocryphal *Life of Adam
and Eve*, and its presence here, in the second leaf of the manuscript, is what
tells you what the compiler was working from.

The making of man follows, and the codex takes it slowly. It states the
doctrine before it makes anything: «not many from father, from son, not many
holy spirit … this Lord all, whole one God» (002v). Then: «Lord, man, heart,
slime of the earth … and became to soul one. Heart breathe on Adam, and living
leave, and man grab Lord, and man go inside Garden of Eden» (002v). That is
Genesis 2:7 — the slime, the breath, the living soul — in the manuscript's own
compressed grammar.

Adam in the garden is «each, every righteously», and «[nor] be hungry and
thirsty» (003r). He is given «one law, this yoke», and the commandment is one
sentence: «through commandment not eat» (003r). Then the sleep, and the rib,
and Eve — and the codex marks the moment with a line of its own devising:
«bone bone, in turn two soul one» (003v).

The serpent is «on this tree what stood in the midst» (003v), and the argument
on that page runs the way it runs in every telling: not «shall not eat» but
«eat», «because it Adam Master through commandment» (003v). Then the fruit,
and «one Adam eat», and «it Adam know» (003v). «Evil and good, how to Lord God
know?» (001r). Their eyes «were opened» «on two», «Adam naked» (001r); they
«see it»; they «be ashamed» (001r).

The interrogation on 001v is one of the places where the manuscript's English
comes out nearly clean. The Lord goes «inside into Paradise» and says, «Adam
why?» (001v). And Adam «hide oneself» from the Lord. And the Lord says, «who
mouth Adam hide oneself» (001v) — who told you. Eve is asked, «why Eve hast
thou done», and answers, and «it serpent it food» (001v). Note what the scribe
does here, and he does it more than once in this book: Adam, caught, «hide
oneself» — the same sign, the same word, that names Lucifer.

The sentence follows: «[cursed] earth» (007r), and Adam «earth till the
earth», and «want to son food» (007r), and what he has now is «painful»
(007r). The Lord's angel, and a «flame sword», and the way «out» (007r); a
«cherub» set «on gate» of the «Garden of Eden» (007v).

And then the codex leaves Genesis and goes into the legend that will carry the
rest of the book. Outside the garden Adam and Eve have three sons — «and
firstborn exist Cain», «and two … exist Abel», «third … exist Seth» (007v) —
and Adam «from eye eye blind» (007v). Blind, he sends his son: «carry Adam
from the tree of mercy» (007v). Seth goes to «gate/open Garden of Eden» and
«God angel» «[answered]» him (006r), and what he is given is «branch» and
«anointed», the anointing that gives light: «through light anointed, through
eye see blind Adam» (006r). The angel «go … inside Garden of Eden and Seth
carry branch» (006r), and Seth carries it home to «father Adam» (006v).

That branch is the seed of everything this manuscript cares about. It is
planted, and it grows, and four hundred leaves later an emperor will take off
his purple and his shoes and carry what was made of it back into Jerusalem on
his bare feet. The compiler knew that when he wrote page six.

## X. The Last Things, and the Writer's Own Days

### folios 221r–224v

The last leaves of the codex stop being a book about the past. They become a
book about the man holding the pen.

They begin with a table. Not a narrative — a list, by weekday, of what the sky
has done and what it means. «Before spirit inside Friday earth one quake» and
«on spirit inside Wednesday moon eclipse one hour» (221v). «Three day before
virgin Mary inside Sunday earth quake» (221v). «[A sign] [shall appear] on
heaven», and «[darkened] [the sun] to inside [the holy church]» (221v); and
after two years, «[famine]» and «[pestilence]» (221v). «[A wind] from spirit
[shall come], earth quake, and moon» (223r). Between the portents the compiler
keeps putting the same two sentences, the ones that run through the whole
manuscript like a drone: «[within] inside righteously believe carry Lord
somebody [shall be saved]», «not believe, and this each, every, therefore have
mercy, somebody [shall perish]» (223r).

Then, on 223v, the book says when it is.

It does not say 1593, and for a while that looked like a quarrel with the
published date. It is not. Read the first line before the number: «from to
leave Lord Jézus Christ from father of Lord out» (223v, line 1) — the count is
from the departure to the Father, the Ascension, not from the Nativity. The
number that follows is «thousand year, five hundred, and six ten year» (223v,
line 2). Put the Ascension at its traditional AD 33 and the sum is **1593**,
which is the year Kiraly and Tokai read in this manuscript by their own route.
The codex and the scholars agree; they were simply counting from different
events, and the codex says which one it is counting from on the line above the
number.

The same page carries a second number, and it is the sharpest external anchor
in the book. The age of the world at the Nativity is given as «five thousand,
and hundred day, and nine ten year, and nine year» (223v, lines 9–10) — 5,199.
That is precisely the figure of the Roman Martyrology's Christmas
proclamation, the number a sixteenth-century Catholic heard read aloud every
Christmas Eve. It is not a number anybody could arrive at by guessing, and
nothing about the method that produced this edition could have invented it.

Two cautions belong here, in the text and not in a footnote. First, both of
those numbers had *also* been restored, separately, as single signs elsewhere
on this same page — guessed from the note saying what the page was expected to
contain. Those two guesses have been withdrawn and now read «[a numeral]»,
because a guess that manufactures the anchor it was supposed to test is worth
nothing. Both numbers survive without them, spelled out sign by sign in
readings that stand on their own. Second, the manuscript's arithmetic is not
tidy even where it is legible: the word «day» is attached to the hundred in
line 9, and line 6 reads «son Moses» where the sense wants something else.
This is a real page of a real book, not a clean one.

The numerals themselves are checkable three ways, and all three are in the
back of this volume. The codex writes the Twelve as «six six» — that phrase
occurs on thirteen lines and nine of them name the apostles. It writes «six
hundred» and «six ten» and «six» twice, at cf. 028v and cf. 159r, and both
times the words beside it are «hell» and «evil»: the number of the beast,
Revelation 13:18. And it spells 5,199 out in pieces that each carry their own
weight.

After the date the disciples ask the obvious question — «Master, when? exist
understand judge day» (223v) — and 222r answers it in the manner of the genre:
«[after] two thousand year» … «die man sin, be damned man sin, and be saved» …
«one one to to earth, water, sun, each, every [shall be shaken]» (222r). That
formula about being saved and damned comes out of the gloss with its polarity
scrambled from folio to folio, and this edition has not straightened it. It is
almost certainly a fixed creedal tag whose word order the rendering is not
capturing. It is named as an open problem in the apparatus rather than quietly
normalised here.

And then, on 222v, the manuscript does the thing that no amount of
decipherment prepares you for. It stops preaching and starts keeping a diary.

Six times on that page stands the sign Kiraly and Tokai gloss as *the name of
the author*.

An earlier draft of this part said it was the only page in four hundred and
forty-one where that sign stands. That was wrong, and it is corrected here
rather than quietly amended: the sign stands on three leaves, cf. 137v, cf. 138r and
222v. On the first two it is inside a prayer to the Virgin, where the compiler
writes himself into the petition — he asks her to pray for his own sins. This
is the only page where he is not praying but recording, and where what he sets
down is where he went and what he did:

> «[on the holy day] Monday inside wound … [I went] to house»
> «brother trespass carry … [to the Lord's house] inside Friday»
> «[and then] … [to the Lord's house] inside Sunday … seal to»
> «holy Philip year, [on the feast of] Monday»
> «[I pray], [my sins], Lord have mercy»
> «Friday [I fasted]»

Two more leaves follow, 224r and 224v, and they are the worst-preserved in the
codex: «Michael on Saturday», «Matthew on Saturday», «[the same week]», «[a
portion] [of wine]», and «one [and a half] three, and one [and a half] three»
(224r). They cite no source, and almost every guess on them is a placeholder;
the apparatus says so. The final lines of the book ask that a soul go «on
high» and «[into heaven]», «soul from lose, from rich [at the last], exist
[amen] [for ever]» (224v).

But the ending that matters is the one on 222v. Whatever else this book is —
compilation, cipher, sermon-cycle, two hundred years of scholarly
embarrassment — a man sat down in 1593, in a script he had built so that
probably only he could read it, and wrote: «Friday [I fasted]». «[I pray], [my
sins], Lord have mercy».

## II. From the Flood to the House of David

### folios 008r–015v

Six leaves carry the manuscript from the ark to the king, and they are in a
hurry. This is not narrative any more; it is a genealogy with three stories
lodged in it.

Noah first. The Lord tells him the flood is coming and that he means to
destroy every one (008r). The ark is given its measurements the way a builder
would give them — the codex says «three hundred» and «cubits long» and
«lifted up» — and then Noah «grab each, every create two by two» and put them
inside (008r). A dove appears. The rain comes, and five towns are destroyed
(008r). And then the compiler includes the detail a devotional book usually
leaves out: after the flood Noah is «drunken» (008r). It is in Genesis 9:21, and
it is here.

Between the flood and Abraham the book pauses to count, and to say who is
telling it. «From Noah» to Abraham the people «were pagans» (008v); and then,
as on every third page of this manuscript, «God's angel to Elijah the prophet»
(008v). The angel adds something about the writing itself: «Elijah the prophet
and «[holy Enoch]» the prophet «write» (008v) — and the brackets there are
not decoration; see the note on Enoch under Part VII. The compiler is telling
you that two
prophets are the authors of what you are reading.

Then Abraham and Isaac, on 005r, and it is one of the best-preserved pages in
the first quarter of the book. The son goes with the father (005r). There is
«one sheep and one lamb» (005r). Isaac is «tie_up» on the place of piercing,
and «[ram]» stands «[instead]» (005r) — one word of that line is a
restoration and it is bracketed here as it is bracketed there. There is a
«sword» and a «slay» (005r), and then «shout» from «on-cloud», from the angel
of the Lord (005r): «leave» Abraham (005r). And the page ends with Abraham
seeing and understanding the «(thorn)bush» (005r).

The codex does not leave that as a story. On the very next leaf it explains
what Abraham was shown, and the explanation is the argument of the whole book:
out of a virgin a son will be born, his name «Jézus», and he will go among the
people and preach the gospel and do many miracles, and «suffer», and be
«crucified», and «on the third day» stand up «from die» (005v). Abraham, in
this book, is shown the Passion. That is why the Rood legend was at the front,
and it is why the codex keeps counting years: it is building a single line from
the tree in Eden to the cross.

The counting is relentless and it is not always coherent. From Abraham to
Moses, from Adam to Abraham, hundreds and thousands of years pile up across
005v and 015v, and the arithmetic does not always survive the rendering. What
survives cleanly is the intent: «from Adam» onward «until» the Virgin Mary
(015v) is a span the book wants you to hold in your head.

David closes the part. He «humbled» himself, he «sin», he was «anointed» by
the Lord, and the Lord «confirmed» him on his «throne» (015r). And then the
line that makes David matter here: the Lord will be born «a son of David», a
son «of God» (015r). From David the count runs on again, to the birth of the
Virgin Mary (015r).

## III. Joachim and Anne, and the Birth of Christ

### folios 016r–022r

Here the codex settles into the rhythm it will keep for four hundred leaves.
A page names its gospel — «write holy-Luke» and a chapter number (016r) — and
then preaches on it.

What it preaches first is not in any gospel. It is the story of Mary's
parents, out of the *Protevangelium* by way of the *Golden Legend*, and the
codex tells it in full. Joachim and Anne divide their substance in three: one
part to «the people of the temple», one to «the Lord's way», and a third for
Joachim and his household to live on (016v). Joachim brings his offering to
the temple after «thirty years» and «the head Jew» sees him and casts him
out (016v). And Joachim, «sadly», leaves, and goes «inside a field», «inside a
forest», and hides on «one mount» (016v).

That is where the angel finds him. Joachim is in the desert and «God appeared»
(017r); the angel «had mercy» on him and sends him home (017r); and at «the
golden gate» Joachim meets his wife Anne (017r, 017v). The gate is named on
both leaves, which is how you know it matters to the compiler: the golden gate
is the image every fifteenth-century altarpiece of this legend uses.

Then the Annunciation, and the codex slows right down for it. «The Lord's
angel Gabriel» comes «to the happy Virgin Mary» (018r), and Holy Luke is cited
by chapter (018r). The greeting comes out of the gloss as «have mercy, girl»
(018r) — the codex's own formula, not Luke's *Ave gratia plena*, and this
edition has not corrected it into Luke's. Mary asks how it can be, since she
«wants to carry as a virgin» (018r). The answer is the Holy Spirit, and the
power that «overshadowed» her (018v), and then Mary's own words: «according to
thy word» (018v). And the angel adds the detail that dates it — Elizabeth,
«six months» gone, «barren» before (018v), who will bear «holy John» (018v).

Joseph enters as «the very old Joseph» (019r), and the codex never once calls
him anything else. It is a fixed epithet, and it belongs to the same tradition
as the refused offering and the golden gate.

The Nativity itself is told plainly. «The emperor Augustus» commands
«all, whole people make census» (019v); Mary and the very old Joseph go back to their
own place (019v); they «can find a room» — which in this grammar means they
could not (020r); they lodge in «a barn» and «a manger» (020r); Joseph buys
«hay» (020r). A son is born «in the night time» (020v), and «he shall be
called Jesus» (020v). There is a «star» over «Bethlehem town» (020v). The
shepherds see it, an angel tells them of «great joy» and that «a king is born»
(020v), and the codex gives the scene its picture: «the ox and donkey», the
«hay», and «inside the manger Christ, Mary's son» (020v). The shepherds go,
and «kneel down» (020v).

Then Egypt. The angel tells the very old Joseph to «stand up» and take «this
son and this son's mother» and «escape into Egypt» (021v). The king's death is
what ends the exile: «[Herod] end» (021v). The name is bracketed on that line
— a restoration, not a reading — though Herod stands plainly, over and over,
when he questions the Lord at cf. 041v, so it is the attribution here and not
the name itself that is uncertain. And when they are in Egypt the
codex reports the apocryphal detail the canonical gospels do not have — in
every town of Egypt «idols» «fall down» (022r).

Two smaller things on these pages are worth a reader's attention. The
circumcision is dated to the eighth day and the name given there (021v). And
at the end of 022r the book counts «six-six apostle» — the codex's way of
writing twelve — and says they «preached» (022r). It writes the Twelve as
six and six in nine places, and once you have seen it you can find it
anywhere in the book.

## IV. The Twelve Signs of His Coming

### folios 022v–028v

This part is a list, and its shape is the most distinctive thing in the first
sixth of the manuscript. The compiler has set himself to enumerate the proofs
that Jesus is «righteously the Son of God», and he numbers them as he goes.

The first series is miracles, counted. Water made «wine» (022v). «Five loaves»
broken for «five thousand» (022v). A widow's son raised «in Nain» (022v). A
healing «within Jerusalem» (022v). Then «in turn seven», a healing «within
Capharnaum», and the sick man is «a soldier», and «the centurion» (022v). Then
«within Tyrus», a woman (022v). «In turn nine» a «paralytic» (023r). «In turn
ten» a «king son» who is «on die» (023r). And at Jerusalem a man with «hell»
in him, «exorcise» (023r). The list runs twelve deep and the numbers are
written out, which is why so much of this part survives: a numeral is the
easiest thing in this manuscript to read and the hardest to fake.

The second series is stranger and better. Having counted the miracles, the
compiler counts the *witnesses* — everyone who ever confessed that this man
was the Son of God — and he does it in order. «Moses and Elijah» «confess»
(023r). The devils «of hell» confess, «because the Lord Jesus is the righteous
Son of God» (025r). «Sky and earth, sun and moon» confess (023v). Abraham the
forefather confesses (026v); holy Anne confesses (026v); the «happy Virgin
Mary» confesses (027r); and «the holy, very old Joseph» confesses (027v). It
is a devotional device — the whole creation called as a witness list — and the
codex sustains it over six leaves.

Inside that frame sit two set pieces. The Transfiguration: the Lord «go Lord Jézus on
Mount Tabor» with the disciples (023v); they see «Moses and Elijah»,
«white» (023v); there is «bright [from heaven]» (023v); the disciples
«get frightened and down fell, bow down» (023v) and hear «a voice» (023v), and the voice
says «beloved son» (024r). And the Baptism, which the codex puts on «Mount
Carmel» rather than at the Jordan (024r) — John «baptize», and the Lord says
«John, baptize the Lord» (024r), and the Holy Spirit appears «inside the shape
of a dove» (024v).

Then the Nativity is told a second time, on 025v, with the star and the
manger and the shepherds all over again. It is not an error and it is not a
scribal duplication: this is a book of readings, and a scene gets told once
where the narrative needs it and again where a sermon needs it. The repetition
runs through the whole manuscript and is discussed in the front matter.

The part ends where the next one will begin. The earth «quaked», «rocks split»
(026r); «the sun and moon eclipse» and «every tree in the world» mourns
(026v). Mary kneels before her son and the book states its doctrine in one
line: «not many gods, but rather one God» (027v). Then the kiss between mother
and son (028r), the lamb prepared «because it was the time of the Jewish
holiday» (028r), and the sentence that opens the largest section of the codex:
«this is begin suffering Lord Jézus Christ» (028r).

One number on 028v deserves its own line, because it is an anchor. Among the
things cast out, the codex writes «six hundred» and «six-ten» and «six»
(028v), and the words standing beside it are «hell» and «evil». It writes the
same number again at cf. 159r, again beside «evil». Six hundred and sixty-six,
twice, both times in the right company: Revelation 13:18.

## V. The Passion

### folios 029r–052v

This is the part of the book that tells you it is a book. It opens by
announcing itself — «begins this begin the account» (029r) — and then, before
a single event, it names its sources: «write the account … holy Matthew and
holy John» (029r). Forty-four leaves follow, and they are the most
continuously legible stretch of the manuscript. Whatever else is uncertain
here, this much is not: the compiler set out to write a Passion harmony out of
two named evangelists, said so at the top, and then did it.

He begins at Bethany. The Lord «go Lord Jézus to Bethany» and then to
Jerusalem, and the apostles are sent ahead to «prepare from Easter lamb»
(029r). They «sit to throne» — the codex's word for taking a place at table —
and the lamb is prepared (029r).

What happens next is the part of the supper the gospels put first, and the
codex gives it four leaves. One of the apostles «carry one bucket water and
one washdish», and «water inside washdish pour», and the Lord goes «to holy
Peter» (029v). Peter refuses: «Master, not let this Peter … foot wash» (030r).
The answer, as the gloss carries it, is about being «cut off» — if you are not
washed you have a «[part]» «cut off» «inside heaven» (030r) — and Peter
reverses himself at once and asks for «[hands and] head» too (030r). Then the
Lord washes, and dries with a «towel», and the page is careful to say that he
washed «each, every» (030r).

One oddity on that page has to be flagged rather than smoothed. The sign the
dictionary glosses as the emperor *Titus* stands twice in the sentence about
the bucket and the washdish (029v). It is almost certainly not the emperor. It
may be a name, it may be a misread of something else, and this edition does
not know; the line is printed at the back as it stands.

Then the bread and the cup, and here the compiler's church shows. He «blessed
… this bread» and sets it «before» them (030v); he takes «wine one cup» and —
this is the detail — «water inside cup pour», and blesses «wine and water»
(030v). Water mixed into the wine at the offertory is Latin-rite practice, not
something either named evangelist describes, and it is on the page. The
apostles «eat and drink», and the codex counts what they ate: «thirty holy
host» (031r).

The betrayal is told by the morsel, which is John's version, not Matthew's.
The Lord «know» that «one among you» (031r); the beloved disciple «lean on» «
on end Lord Jézus» (031r); the sign is given — «to whom this Lord give bite»
(031r); and then «bread exist Lord Judas Iscariot» (031v). What enters him,
the codex says in its own vocabulary, is «hell bad thing inside Judas go»
(031v). Judas «rise … and go Jew(ish) head inside Jerusalem», and the price is
«thirty silver» (032r). The manuscript dates it: «Lord exist inside Wednesday»
(032r). Spy Wednesday is a liturgical fact, not a gospel one, and the compiler
had it.

Peter is warned in the same breath: «before cock … Lord to three exist deny»
(032v).

The garden is reached by name. They cross «one brook Kidron» (033r) — the
codex spells it both ways, Kidron here and «Cedron» two lines later (033r) —
and three are taken apart: «Peter and … John and James» (033r). The distance
is Luke's: «stone to throw» (033v). He kneels — «kneel down Lord Jézus» — and
prays «father of Lord, God heaven … not take father from Lord this suffering»
(033v). He comes back and the apostles «to sleep» (033v). The second time the
prayer has the clause that makes it a prayer and not a refusal: «[thy will]
pleasing» (034r). And the sweat is there, with the Greek word behind it
showing through the restoration: «to to to sweat through Lord Jézus», «[in an
agony]» (034r). Then «go angel from the eternal high» (034v) — Luke's
comforting angel, which only Luke has, in a book that said it was following
Matthew and John.

The arrest is the codex at its most vivid, and it is John's arrest. Judas
«ask for sign … with kiss» (035r); he «go Judas against Lord Jézus and kiss»
(035r). Then the question, three times: «Lord Jézus, who(m)? search» (035v),
and the answer «Jézus Nazareth» (035v), and «each, every Jew(ish) back bow
down» (035v) — they go over backwards, which is in John and nowhere else. The
sequence is written out three times on three successive leaves, once per cry,
with the wording barely varied. That is not scribal duplication. It is a
compiler who wanted the threefold shape on the page.

Then the sword. «cut off with sword ear one Jew(ish)», and the man «was named
… Malchus» (036v) — John is the only evangelist who names him. The Lord «give
… this ear», and «ear … put on place», and «healing ear» (036v). It is a
miracle only Luke reports and only John gives a name to, and this page has
both halves.

From there the book moves in the rhythm of a Passion read aloud. He is «tie
up» by the «hand» and «[led]» «to Jew(ish) head» (037r); on the way over the
Kidron he «on bridge fall down» and is «[struck]» (037r) — the fall from the
bridge is legend, not gospel, and it is one of the places where the compiler's
devotional sources come through the harmony. Peter denies him at the gate.
Caiaphas gives his counsel, and the codex renders it almost as a proverb: it
is «good that one Lord die» rather than «all the world» perish (038v).

Pilate takes four leaves, and Herod three. Herod «[mocked]» him and sent him
back «in a white garment» (042v); he had «hoped» to see «a miracle» «done»
(043r) — Luke again. Pilate has him «whip», «from [pillar] flog» (043v); the
soldiers put on the «purple robe» and the «thorn crown on head», sit him on a
«chair», kneel, and say «healing Jézus» (044v) — *Ave*, hail, the mock
homage. He is given «[blows]» (044v). Pilate «carry … water inside one
washdish» and washes, and says he is «innocent from of Lord blood» (047v).
Barabbas is released and Jesus condemned (047v). Pilate goes out on the house
and shouts «lo Jézus Nazareth king Jew(ish)» (046r) — *Ecce homo* and the
*titulus* in one sentence, twice.

One exchange on 045v is worth stopping at, because it is not in the harmony's
two named gospels either. Asked whether he is king, the answer given is that
he could «ask» of «from father God» «six six an army in turn angel» (045v) —
six and six is this book's way of writing twelve, and twelve legions of angels
is Matthew's line in Gethsemane, moved here into the hearing before Pilate.
The compiler is working from a Passion he knew as a whole, not copying a
column.

Then the manuscript does the thing that most clearly marks it as a book made
for Holy Week. On 048r the narrative stops and a voice begins to complain:
«[O my] this people to good [what] then exist Lord this Lord» — what good have
I done thee, my people. And the complaint is answered with a history: «this
people chapter go on Egypt [out of] living servant» (048r); «over sea» and it
«divide» (048r); the people are «[fed]» «inside field» with «angel bread»
(048v); and in return, «[thou hast prepared] cross» (048v). That is the
*Improperia*, the Reproaches sung at the veneration of the cross on Good
Friday. Its presence tells you what this manuscript was for.

The count of years in the wilderness on that page reads «two two ten year»
(048v). This edition does not resolve it. The codex elsewhere writes forty as
ten and ten and ten and ten (cf. 004v), so the wilderness figure here is not
in the form the book uses for forty, and no reading is asserted.

The way to the cross runs «over exist Cedron» again (049v), which is where the
garden was; the compiler has brought the road back past it. He «fall down»
(049v). «Lord Simon cross carry» (049v). The garments are «[they parted]»
(049v). On the hill he is laid on the wood: «place onto cross» (050r), and
then «pierce», and «chain draw», and «foot pierce» (050r, 050v). The nails are
counted — «three nails», «long» (051v). The title is written by Pilate «on one
tablet»: «Jézus Nazareth king Jew(ish)» (050v), and the codex knows there was
an argument about it, because the Jews «write Lord king Jew(ish)» and Pilate
writes it his own way anyway (050v). It knows the title was in «[three
tongues]» (050v). It has «two thief» (050v). It has «nine hour» (050v). It has
the thirst, and «vinegar and [hyssop]», and the «sponge … on a stick» (051v),
and «mouth little on pine» — a small mouthful of something bitter (051v).

Then: «why in turn, and from to Lord Jézus of Lord commend, give up the ghost.
End this the account, Passion, evangelist, suffering Lord Jézus» (052r). The
compiler closes his harmony with a colophon, in the middle of the story.

And then he carries on for one more leaf, because two things still have to
happen. «Earth quake, rock stone rent, sun and moon this eclipse», and «each,
every create mourn» (052v). And a soldier: «one soldier on Jerusalem blind»,
«was named soldier exist Longinus», who «pierce» with a «spear», and whose
blindness is cured by what comes out — «through see and healing soldier» —
after which he «believe Lord Jézus Christ» and is «baptize» (052v). Longinus
blind and then seeing is the *Golden Legend*, not the gospels. The dictionary
glosses the sign as Longinus in its own entry, so the name here is Kiraly and
Tokai's, not this edition's.

## VI. The Burial, the Resurrection, and the Forty Days

### folios 053r–009v

This part ends on two leaves numbered in the nines. That is not an error. The
codex was rebound at some point by somebody who could not read it, and the
Thomas pericope that closes this section sits physically near the front of the
volume. This edition follows the reading order, not the binding, and says so
here because the reader will notice.

It begins in the dark. «[Darkness] [came]» (053r); the crowd goes «home» «from
[breast] [striking]» (053r), beating their breasts, which is Luke's detail and
nobody else's. The hours are counted from «nine hour» (053r).

Then two men. «Two somebody have mercy» in Jerusalem, and one «was named …
Joseph», and the other is «Nicodemus» (053v), and the two «ask for from
Pilate» for the body (053v). They «go down [from the cross]» (054r). The three
nails come out — «three three nails» (054r). And then the sentence that a
fifteenth-century reader would have recognised as a picture before it was a
sentence: they «grab virgin Mary inside bosom» (054r). The body is laid in the
mother's lap. The codex does not linger on it, and this edition will not
either, but it is the *pietà*, and it is on the page.

Nicodemus brings «[linen]» and they «cover» the body (054r). He is put «inside
burial chamber», which is «close»d, and they «leave seal» and set «two two
soldier» on it (054r). And the veil: «[the veil] go [part]» (054r). Then the
formula that ends a reading: «end this holy gospel» (054r).

The next leaf is a single line, a rubric and nothing else: «begins this holy
gospel write holy Mark» (054v). The compiler is starting a new lesson, and he
tells you whose.

The women come at once. The codex calls them «the three Marys», as one word,
and then names them: «Mary Salome», «and Mary James mother», «and Mary
Magdalene» (055r). They have «[bought]» «[spices]» to «[anoint]» him (055r).
They find the stone «from [rolled away]» (055r). Inside is «angel sit on left
(side) direction», «from [a young man]» (055v) — Mark's young man sitting on
the right, and the codex has put him on the left; the difference is left
standing, not corrected. Mary is «startle»d and «supposed» it was «a ghost»
(055v). The angel says «do not … [be affrighted]» (055v), and sends them: «go
… inside Galilee», «say … of Lord disciple and Peter» (055v). *And Peter* is
Mark's phrase, and this manuscript has it.

Magdalene goes back alone. «Appear Lord Jézus Mary Magdalene inside shape,
form from one gardener» (056r) — she takes him for the gardener, which is
John's scene. She «stooped down» and looked into the tomb (056r), and there
was «light from gate» (056r). She turns: «Mary [turning]» (056r). The page
calls her what the medieval west called her, «this woman who … came … mourn»,
and this edition notes that the identification of Magdalene as the penitent
woman is the compiler's tradition, not the gospels'. Then she is sent: «to
Mary apostle go Lord apostle» (056r). Apostle to the apostles, in the sign the
codex uses for apostle.

He stands among them — «commandment God among you» (057r) — and the lesson
closes again on «this apostle holy gospel» (057r).

Then the compiler does something that no gospel does, and it takes three
leaves. He goes down into hell after him.

First he lays the groundwork out of the Old Testament, under the name of a
prophet whose sign the dictionary cannot fix — Kiraly and Tokai gloss it only
as «holy» plus *the name of a prophet*, and this edition will not guess which
one. The text says «exist find inside» the old scriptures «righteously write
this word: stand up Lord, on die Lord» (057v), and then: «destroy Lord evil
Christ, and from [Adam] [bound] evil» (057v). Harrowing prophecy, and the
compiler has gone looking for it.

Then the descent itself. The soul «go … on evil and evil destroy» (058r). The
dead have lain «inside one hundred year» and more (058r). «Each, every
prophet» is there (058r). And he «[brought out] three soul», and «each, every
out go» with the Lord — but «in turn three soul inside evil stay» (058r).
Three brought out and three left behind: the codex says it twice, in the same
line, and this edition does not know what the three are. It is printed as it
stands.

On the next leaf the compiler cites «holy Augustine» «church father» (058v)
for what happens when the freed souls come up, and then writes the scene this
part is worth reading for. The Lord's soul comes out, and appears «to happy
virgin Mary», and «kneel down Adam soul before … happy virgin Mary», and «girl
… have mercy», and he «ask for» and «bless» her (058v). Adam kneels to the
Virgin. That is not in Genesis, not in the gospels and not in the *Life of
Adam and Eve*; it is the whole argument of the book compressed into one
sentence, the first man doing reverence to the woman through whom his own
fall was undone. It is the best thing in this part of the manuscript.

The hours are counted again and the arithmetic does not close: «two ten ten
five hour» and then «thirty … six hour» (058v). This edition leaves it.

Then a single line on its own leaf — «on the third day from die stand up Lord
Jézus Christ» (059r) — and a new rubric: «begins this holy gospel write holy
Luke» (059v).

Emmaus takes five leaves and is the best-preserved sustained narrative outside
the Passion. «Two apostle» go out from Jerusalem to a place «day name exist
Emmaus» (059v). They «talk» (059v). He joins them «shape, form traveller»
(059v) — in the form of a traveller, which is the *Golden Legend*'s pilgrim,
not Luke's bare *another form*. He asks; they answer with a rebuke —
«[knowest not] … miracle Jerusalem» (060r) — and tell him about the man who
«do» «miracle» and was «crucify» by the «head» (060r). They list what he did:
the «blind» seeing «through light», the dead «resurrect», the «lame» «healing»
(060v). And they say the thing Luke has them say, that some «apostle» heard
the news and did not believe (060v).

Then the codex names him, and this is worth saying plainly, because it is the
kind of detail that cannot be got by guessing: «and then Cleopas, Luke, this
Lord to two [foolish]» (061r). Cleopas is named, in the right pericope, in a
gospel the page has already told you is Luke's, next to the word the scene
turns on: *O foolish*. The word «[slow of heart]» stands two lines later
(061r).

And then the compiler cannot help himself. Luke says only that he expounded
the scriptures beginning at Moses; this book gives the exposition. «From ~Adam
… scripture» (061r). Abel: «Abel symbolize this Jézus execute», and the
reason, «die of brother» — killed by his brother (061r). Noah (061r). Then
Abraham and Isaac at length: Abraham «symbolize this Jézus execute» (061v);
Isaac carries «[the wood]» (061v); Abraham «tie up faggot on Isaac» and takes
«sword» and «want slay» (061v); they «go far» with «donkey» (062r); there is
«one sheep and one lamb» (062r); and the «shout Lord in the cloud» stops him —
«go away Abraham» (062r). The point is stated: «to the whole wide world …
Lord … love» (062r). Abraham is the Father and Isaac is the Son, and the codex
says so on the next line: «to grab of father this, and this Jézus execute»
(062v).

Then Luke's ending, kept exactly. He «have … long way» and makes to go on; the
two «persuade» him (062v); they «sit … to throne» (062v); he takes «one cup»
and «[he blessed]» (063r); and then «[vanished]» (063r). The lesson closes:
«end this holy gospel» (063r).

The last rubric of the part names John and his chapter: «begins this holy
gospel write holy John, within two ten ten chapter leave» (063v) — twenty, in
the codex's way of writing it, which is the right chapter. «Holy Thomas
Didymus» comes «one Saturday evening» (063v) and refuses: «this not believe»
unless «Thomas of Lord side» and «of Thomas finger» «put within» (063v). The
doors are «closed», and «peace you exist» (009r). And then the offer, word for
word: «go … to put of Thomas finger within of Lord wound» (009r).

What the codex does with the answer is its own. Thomas «kneel down» (009r).
He says «Lord of Thomas God of Thomas» (009r) — my Lord and my God — and then
he asks for something John does not give him: «have mercy Thomas, who this
Thomas through sin against this Lord» (009r). He confesses. And the Lord's
reply is John's, in two halves the codex keeps apart: «happy … somebody see
and believe», «but and happy … food see … believe» (009r) — blessed are they
that have seen, and blessed are they that have not seen and have believed.

Then the part ends by opening the next one. Thomas «bless Lord Jézus Christ»
(009v), and the lesson closes, and a new rubric begins at once: «write holy
John, inside ten chapter» (009v), and the Lord says to the apostles at dinner,
«this Lord good son» (009v). The Good Shepherd, John's tenth chapter, and the
long middle of the book has started.

> translation. Every sentence is drawn from the folios cited beside it; words
> inside guillemets are the manuscript's own, as Book Two renders them,
> brackets and all, and a bracketed word there is a restoration rather than a
> reading. Nothing has been supplied from the gospels, from Augustine or from
> the *Golden Legend* — where those are named above it is to say what a thing
> on the page resembles, which is an editorial judgment and is marked as one.

## VII. The Preaching: the Sermons and the Parables

### folios 064r–182v

Two hundred and thirty-five leaves — more than half the manuscript — and they
are not a narrative. They are a book of readings. The shape repeats so
regularly that once you have seen it twice you can find your place anywhere in
this part: a rubric naming the evangelist and the chapter, «begins this holy
gospel, write holy Luke» (cf. 059v) and the like; then the pericope; then a sermon
on it, often with a second story attached; then «end this holy gospel» (064v)
and the
next rubric. The compiler is working through a lectionary.

What makes this part worth reading rather than merely counting is that the
sermons are not padding. When the codex reaches a parable it tells it at
length, allegorises it, and then tells you what each piece stands for — and
its allegories are the sixteenth-century Latin west's, not anything a modern
reader would invent. The best writing in the manuscript is here.

It is presented below in five stretches. The divisions are this edition's, not
the book's; the book runs straight through.

#### The last discourse, the Ascension, and Pentecost (064r–082v)

It opens mid-sentence, because the rubric was on the leaf before: «of Lord
sheep, and Lord know of Lord sheep» (064r). The Good Shepherd, John's tenth
chapter. And at once the compiler does the thing he will do for two hundred
leaves — he turns the image into a story with people in it. There is «one
king» who «have two shepherd»: «one … home good shepherd», and «in turn two
farm hand shepherd» (064r) — the shepherd of the household and the hired one.
The wolf comes; the hired shepherd «leave this sheep»; the good one «redeem
this sheep» (064r). Then back to the text: «good shepherd … put down of Lord
living to of Lord sheep» (064v) — he layeth down his life for the sheep —
and «one [fold]» (064v), and «hear voice, sound of Lord … sheep» (064v).

Then a run of short readings, each with its rubric. Matthew's seventh chapter:
«false prophet», in «evil [clothing]», who are «[ravening wolves]» (065r).
Mark's sixteenth, twice over: «somebody … believe … be saved», «each, every
somebody be damned» (067v). The great commandment, which this book gives in
Paul's mouth rather than the lawyer's: «love Lord most high, all, whole heart,
and all, whole somebody how to neighbour» (068r). That is set out as three
askings of «holy Paul apostle» (067v, 068r), and then the whole passage is
written out a second time thirty leaves later on 071r, word for word. The
manuscript does this repeatedly, and it is not damage; it is a compiler
copying the same reading into two places in his year.

John's sixteenth chapter gets two leaves and is one of the clearest pages in
the book. «This Lord go of Lord father» (068v); «this Lord you go [the
Paraclete]» (068v); and the threefold reproof, counted out in the codex's
numbering: «first from sin, in turn two from righteously, third judge» (068v).
Of sin, and of righteousness, and of judgment. The whole verse is there, in
order, under a rubric that names John and the sixteenth chapter. That is the
citation test this project uses to check itself, and this page passes it
outright.

Then the vine. «Farm, in turn you vine branch» (069v); the Father is the
husbandman, «farm» (069v); a branch that bears nothing is «cut off» and
«throw out» (069v, 070r); and where it goes is the codex's standing formula
for hell, «fire there exist, see grinding tooth, crying, oh, oh» (070r). The
weeping and gnashing of teeth, in a book that writes its own exclamations.

John's sixteenth again, on the sorrow that turns: a woman in travail, «one
baptize head son be born», and after the birth «joy», and «many sad(ly)» is
«[shall be turned]» (071v). Your sorrow shall be turned into joy.

Mark's last chapter carries the Ascension, and the compiler gives it its
setting first: «sit apostle at table inside Jerusalem» (072r) — at meat, which
is the Douay's word and the manuscript's, read here from Kiraly and Tokai's
own citations of that sign and not from any pool of English candidates. Then
the signs that shall follow: «evil inside somebody exorcise», «serpent carry
inside hand», «deadly poison drink» and be «not ill», the sick «from healing»
(073r). Then: «[taken up] Lord Jézus», and he is «take … to eternal glory»
(074r).

The Lord's Prayer stands on 074v, whole, in the manuscript's own word order:
«of Lord son as father of somebody, and Lord, inside eternal, be hallowed name
of father»; «go man inside kingdom of father»; «exist the whole wide world of
Lord, how heaven this and earth»; «bread of somebody each, every day, grab
Lord man today's day»; «this somebody trespass forgive, how to man forgive of
somebody»; «go man inside pleasing chapter, redeem from evil, amen» (074v).
Our Father, hallowed be thy name, thy kingdom come, thy will be done in heaven
and in earth, our daily bread, forgive us our trespasses as we forgive, lead
us not into temptation, deliver us from evil. Seven petitions and a doxology,
in the right order, on one leaf. The page names its source in the next line:
«write holy Matthew inside of gospel» (074v).

Then the two men in white. «Appear two angel angel, white clothes» (075r), and
they ask the apostles why they stand «[gazing]» (075v), and say he «go on …
judge living and die man» (075v) — shall so come, to judge the quick and the
dead. The creed's clause, in the scene the creed took it from.

Pentecost takes three leaves and the compiler has clearly thought about it.
The apostles «stand … prayer» with «virgin Mary» (080v) for «ten year» — the
codex's word for the ten days (080v). Then «from sky go holy spirit» (081r),
«on spirit fire shape, form» (081v), and «[cloven tongues]» (081v), and the
form of a «dove» «inside fire» (081v). The Jews come «on see» because the
noise is heard (082r); the apostles speak «various language» (082r); and the
count is exact: «three thousand» «grab believe Lord Jézus Christ» and are
«[baptized]» (082r, 082v). Three thousand souls, which is Acts and no gospel,
in a book that has just cited Luke's second chapter for the scene (080v).

And then, without a break, the compiler starts a sermon on the Trinity, and it
runs for six leaves: «this holy spirit [proceedeth] on father … go out spirit,
in turn son» (082v).

#### The Trinity, Augustine, and the rich man (083v–099v)

The Trinity sermon does what every medieval preacher on the Trinity does: it
reaches for the sun. «Light symbolize son God, in turn warmth symbolize holy
spirit, in turn from Sun symbolize from father» (083v). One body, three
things, and none of them the others: «he is sun, one shape, form» (083v).

Then the compiler tells the story that everyone who has read anything about
Saint Augustine knows, and tells it well. «Go holy Augustine shore sea»,
because he wanted to «understand» «three» — «father, son, spirit, one God»
(084r). «[One day at] evening», he finds «one little son» «on shore» (084r),
«sit» «[on the sand]» (084r), «[digging] one pit» (084r), with «one spoon» in
his «hand» (084r), scooping the sea into the hole. Augustine asks what he is
doing. And the child answers first — «say this little son» (084v) — and the
answer, as the manuscript has it, is that Augustine will sooner do this than
«[understand]» and «write» «[the Trinity]» (084v). Then «[cannot] see word
afterward before holy Augustine» (084v): the child is gone.

It is one of the two or three best-preserved set pieces in the codex, and the
sign this edition once read as *child* in it is not *child*. It is Kiraly and
Tokai's own sign for *a little while; little*, standing two lines above ours on
the same folio, and the correction is recorded in the apparatus. The boy is
«little» throughout.

The sermon that follows quotes an apostle by name and gets the argument right:
«speak holy James» (084v), and «among you through transgress one law God to
commandment, all, whole … [in one point]» — «[guilty of all]» (085r). Whosoever
shall offend in one point is guilty of all. James, chapter two, verse ten, and
the compiler has both the citation and the sense.

Elijah returns for three leaves with a torch. He «kneel down» and «pray» and
«fire [from heaven]» makes «one [torch]» (086r), and the angel tells him the
torch «symbolize» the Lord (086r). Then the allegory is spelled out: the torch
«symbolize» «to happy virgin Mary», because from her «get conceived» the Lord
(086v), and the fire is «[unconsumed]» (086v). The bush that burns and is not
burnt, read as the Virgin who bears and remains a virgin. That reading is
standard in the Latin west and the compiler states it flatly, twice.

Then, at the bottom of 088r, the manuscript says a word that dates and places
it more sharply than almost anything else in the book. Of the soul after
death: «on die, soul inside purification fire until judge day» (088r).
Purgatory, named, in the ordinary devotional sense, in a Christian book of
1593 — which is the single most Catholic sentence in four hundred and
forty-one leaves, and one a Protestant compiler of that decade would not have
written.

It stands where it stands because the next reading is Dives and Lazarus, and
the compiler has read the parable as a lesson about almsgiving and the state
of souls. Luke's sixteenth chapter is named in the rubric (088v). Then: «one
rich somebody», clothed in «[linen] and purple» (088v), who «from day until
day» is in «joy» and «[fared sumptuously]» (088v). And «one Lazarus» at the
«house», «from head until toe covered wound» (088v), who «ask for» «alms»
(088v, 089r) and is «[full of sores]» (089r) and wants «crumbs» that «[fell
from]» the «table» (089r). The «dog» come and «lick» his «wound» (089r).

Then he dies: «this Lazarus die, go angel heaven glory», and the angels «grab»
him and «carry» him «inside öl Abraham forefather» (089r). That last word is
worth stopping on. *Öl* is the Hungarian for a lap or a bosom, and it has come
through the dictionary untranslated — Kiraly and Tokai gloss into Hungarian,
and this is one of the few places where their Hungarian reaches the page
unconverted. Abraham's bosom, in the language of the people who read this book
first.

The rich man dies too, and is «bury», and «suffer inside evil» (089v). He
«[lifted up his eyes]» (089v) and sees Lazarus «inside öl father Abraham»
(089v), and asks for the thing the parable is famous for: Lazarus's «little
finger» to «immerge water» and «cool» his «tongue» (089v). The answer is
Abraham's: «this rich, good [in thy lifetime]», and Lazarus «[evil things]»,
«[now]» reversed (089v). Between them is «great chasm» which none may «[pass
over]» (090r). And then the ending, which the codex renders almost exactly:
he has «prophet» and their «preach» (090r), and if he will not hear them,
«neither» will he believe «and body from die stand up» (090v) — though one rose
from the dead.

The next rubric names John's third chapter, and the book gets it wrong: it
writes «second chapter» (090v). This is the one failure in the ten citation
checks this project has run, and it is left on the record here rather than
corrected, because a compiler who got nine right and one wrong is a person
working from memory, and that is evidence about what kind of book this is.

The reading itself is right. «Go Nicodemus inside night to Lord Jézus» (090v),
«because have Jew(ish) and not want» to be seen going (090v) — the codex
supplies the motive John leaves out. Then: «man cannot exist to Lord believe,
born again, second be born on this world» (091r); Nicodemus's objection, «how
this can exist» to go «second from of mother» (091r); and the answer, «second
be born … from water and from holy spirit» (091v). And then John's most
quoted verse, in the manuscript's grammar: «you love father of Lord God
heaven» and «of father only one son Jézus» (091v), «and one somebody be saved»
(092r), «[only begotten]» (092r). Then the sentence about the dark: «not want
on light go, a) darkness love» (092v). Men loved darkness rather than light.

Luke's fourteenth chapter follows, and the Great Supper is one of the pages
where the compiler's habit of naming his allegory pays. «One rich man» makes
«many dinner» (092v) and sends his «living servant» (093r). The excuses come
in order and they are Luke's: the first has «buy» «plough land» and must «go
see» it (093r); the second has bought «five yoke … ox» and wants to «[try]»
them, and says «[pray thee] [excused]» (093v); the third has «marry» and
cannot come (094r). Then the servant is sent «on (on the) roadside and on
way», «and on town and on gate town» (094r), to bring in the «blind eye», the
«[maimed] body», the «be hungry and thirst» (094r). And a guest at the table
calls out the line that ends the reading: «blessed» is he who shall eat in the
«heaven kingdom» (094v).

The Bread of Life runs from 095r to 097v and the compiler has put the
institution of the supper inside it, mixed cup and all: «wine one cup and
water inside cup pour», «blessed» (095v). «This bread eat» (095v). The Jews
«strive» over it — «eat^ Jew(ish) who strive» (096r) — which is John's word.
«Of Lord body eat» (096r). «This is indeed eat and … this is indeed drink»
(096r). «[Everlasting life]» (096r). «That is bread living, he who go … from
the eternal» «on this world» (096v). And the communion formula the codex
repeats in several places, with its own number in it: «from altar (table)
exist from thirty holy host eat» (097v).

On 097r the script does something no other page in the book does. The whole
company of heaven — Lord, Father, Son, God, Jesus, Holy Spirit, Mary, Christ,
apostle, angel — is written as a single compound sign, and the renderer prints
it as one word, four times on the leaf (097r). It stands where John says *he
that abideth in me, and I in him*; the gloss carries «[abideth in me]» on the
same page (097r). A scribe who can compress ten names into one glyph is not
encrypting. He is abbreviating, and he is doing it in the place where the
liturgy names them all together.

The stretch ends with the epistle rather than the gospel: «man grab and God
man grab [abideth]» (099r), and then the hard sentence, twice — if a man «say
this somebody God love» and yet «hateth» his brother, he is «[a liar]» (099r,
099v).

#### Elijah, Jerusalem, and the parables of what was lost (100r–121r)

Elijah comes back, and the compiler treats him as history. He is «take» by
«fire on heaven» (100r), and then the page lists what he did in the same
formula it uses for the Lord's miracles — «blind sight», «die» «raised up»,
«lame body», the «obsessed by the evil» «from healing» (100r). Then it does
something a devotional book rarely bothers to do: it names its authorities.
«Church father» «pagan», «scholar», «Pharisees», «and church father this three
write», and «Saint Augustine the church father» (100v). The compiler is
telling you he has sources and that they disagree.

Elijah and Enoch are then kept back for the end. He is «get raptured» to
«heaven most high» (101r); «[Enoch] leave to leave» (101r); and what they are
kept for is «[Antichrist]», who is «[of a harlot] through be born» and is the
«son» of «hide oneself angel» (101r) — Lucifer's son by a harlot, which is the
standard Antichrist legend of the late middle ages and is nowhere in
scripture. The reading «of a harlot» was itself a correction: an earlier guess
made the line say *shall be born* beside *be born*, and a mechanical sweep for
restorations that repeat their neighbour caught it.

**Enoch has to carry a warning, and it is the clearest case in this book of
what a bracket costs.** The name stands three times in the manuscript — cf. 008v,
101r and cf. 133v — and every one of the three is bracketed. It is a
restoration in all three places and a reading in none of them. So the sentence
above, that Enoch and Elijah are the two kept back for the Antichrist, rests
entirely on a guess this project made, and it is a guess the legend itself
makes easy to make: anyone who knows the tradition knows who the second
witness is, which is exactly the circumstance in which a restoration is worth
least. Elijah is read. Enoch is supplied. The distinction is invisible in a
translation and it is the whole difference between the two names.

Two leaves on Simeon follow, and the codex's image of him is the one from the
feast: «from Simeon carry bosom Lord Jézus Christ, [took him]» (102r). Then
the ministry in summary: «inside thirty year» he goes «from town until town,
from temple until temple, from plough land until plough land» (102r).

The destruction of Jerusalem takes three leaves and it is the most
historically specific passage in the manuscript. The Lord «crying this
Jerusalem» (110r) because «[thine enemies]» shall «surround» and «[straiten
thee]» (110r), and «[stone] » upon stone «shall not be left» (110v). Then the
names: «the Roman on head», «Vespasi-» «-anus», «son Titus» (110v), who are
«from father son» (111v), and the year is counted from the crucifixion (111v).
And then the detail that can only have come from Josephus, directly or at
several removes: in the famine, «one say hunger die», and another asks «how
shall we» and eats «of son» (111v). A mother eating her child in the siege.
Beside it the compiler puts a piece of arithmetic he clearly relished: the
captives were sold «on thirty to one denarius» — thirty for a penny —
«because» «Judas» «vend» for thirty (112r).

The cleansing of the temple is on the same leaf, with John's whip in it:
«within Jerusalem temple», «them that sold», «seller of doves», «of cords»
«scourge», «[drove out]» (110v), and «this is prayer house» (110v).

Then the Good Samaritan, and here the compiler's method is on full display.
The lawyer asks, and is made to recite the answer himself — «love Lord most
high, each, every heart, all, whole of somebody soul, all, whole of somebody
might» and «how to somebody neighbour» (105r) — and then asks «who? exist»
his neighbour (105v).

And the man who went down to Jericho is not a man. The gloss names him, on
every line he appears on, as **Adam**. He goes «on field to one place» called
«Jericho» (105v); he «fell among robber» (105v); they «grab booty» and «beat»
him and leave him «half dead and half alive» (106r). A «descendant» of Abraham
passes «long» him — goes the long way round — and so does a second (106r).
Then «go on one Samaritan» who «have mercy to Adam», and «pour wine» into «of
Adam wound» and «bound up» them, and «place» him «on of Lord shoulder» and
carries him «on lodging» and gives the «innkeeper» «two [pence]» (106v). Then
the question is turned back: «who? … this good», and the Jew answers, «he is
[neighbour]» (107r).

The compiler then explains it, and cites his source by name for the
explanation: «speak Saint Augustine the church father, [two pence],
[Testaments]» — «[the Old]», «[the New]» (107v). The two pence are the two
Testaments. That is Augustine's reading, attributed to Augustine, on the page.
And the wounded man being Adam is the same tradition's: the traveller is
mankind, Jericho is the world, the Samaritan is Christ, the inn is the Church.
The manuscript does not spell every step of that out. It spells out enough of
it — Adam by name, the two Testaments by Augustine — that the rest is not a
guess about what a sixteenth-century compiler meant.

The Prodigal Son gets four leaves and is the best writing in the codex. It
begins with a rubric that misnames its evangelist — «write holy Matthew inside
one chapter» (113r) over a parable that is Luke's fifteenth — and then never
puts a foot wrong again.

And the younger son, like the traveller to Jericho, is named for what he
stands for. The gloss calls him **soul-son** throughout: the soul. He «ask
for» his «divide into parts» (113r, 113v); he goes «far inside town» (113v);
he «prodigalize» and «lived riotously» (113v); the famine comes and he is «be
hungry» (113v); he goes to «one pigman» (113v) and would eat «[husks]» of the
«[swine]» (114r). Then: «begin soul son speak, have from father … farm hand
and servant good», bread «tasty» and enough, «a) this soul son» starves
(114r). I will arise and go to my father.

What he has wasted is named too, and it is not money. The codex writes it as a
single compound twice: «eye, say, hear, love, have mercy, believe,
righteously» — «five sense» (113v, 116r). The substance the soul squandered is
its own senses.

The father «see far» him (114v); the son «kneel down» and says «through sin»
against «this father and against» the Lord (114v). And then the robe, which is
where the manuscript stops paraphrasing anyone and starts preaching: «carry»
«the most beautiful robe», and the robe is «love», and it is «have mercy», and
it is «righteously» (115r). Three namings of one garment, in three successive
clauses. The best robe is charity, mercy and righteousness. That is the
compiler's own voice, and it is the finest sentence in four hundred and
forty-one leaves.

The elder brother is «[in the field]» and hears «[music]» and «[dancing]» and
«[asked]» what it was (115v). And the ending is Luke's, kept: «exist soul son
was lost», «exist die and rise on die be saved» (116r).

Then John the Baptist, on a mountain the codex insists on: «preach holy John
the Baptist on Carmel to mount» (116v). Not the Jordan. Carmel is Elijah's
mountain, and this book has just spent three leaves on Elijah; the compiler
has run the forerunner and the prophet together, and he does it more than
once. Four groups come to him and each asks the same question — «soldier»,
«Pharisee», «farm», and «sinners» — and each asks «how shall we be saved»
(116v, 117r, 118r). The soldiers are told to «donate» and be «[content]»
(117r). The sinners are given the great commandment entire: «love Lord», «all,
whole heart», «all, whole … soul», «all, whole … might», «[with all thy
strength]», and «how to somebody neighbour» (118r).

The part ends with the two parables of what was lost, and with the reading
that makes sense of why they are here. «One hundred sheep» and one «lose»;
the shepherd «[layeth it]» on his «[shoulders]» and goes to «friend and
neighbours» and says «found … sheep [which was lost]» (119r, 119v). Then «one
woman» with «ten (piece of) silver» who «loseth one», lights a «lamp», and
«find» it (119v).

And then: «Lord nine order angel inside heaven land» (120r). There are nine
orders of angels, and there was a tenth. «On angel name exist hide oneself
angel» (120r) — Lucifer, who prayed «ten ten ten ten» days and nights (120r,
120v) and fell, and his «one order» went with him. So «[the tenth] from order
on place [shall stand empty]» «until judge year» (120v) — and it is to be
filled: «[shall fill it]» (120v), by the «righteously people» who «believe»
(121r). The lost sheep and the lost drachma are the tenth order of angels, and
the ninety-nine who never strayed are the nine who never fell.

That reading is Gregory the Great's, and it is one of the most characteristic
things in medieval preaching. The manuscript does not name Gregory. It simply
uses him, which is what a compiler does with something everybody in his
audience already knew.
