# Repeated formulas used by the reading pass

This table fixes only phrases whose sense is explicit in Király and Tokai's
dictionary. It is evidence for the reading pass, not text printed in Book One.
The English is plain and remains the same wherever the same formula occurs.

## Gospel boundaries

Gloss: `end this holy-gospel`

English: `Here ends this holy gospel.`

Evidence: K&T's entry for the first sign calls the complete expression an
"ending formula of text units" and prints it as "[here] ends this Holy
Gospel." The entries for the next signs give `this`, `holy`, and `gospel`.

Gloss: `begins this holy-gospel`

English: `Here begins this holy gospel.`

Evidence: K&T's entry for the first sign calls the complete expression a
"beginning formula of textual units" and prints it as "[here] begins this
Holy Gospel," with 177v03 as an example.

Gloss: `begins this holy-gospel write holy-[evangelist]`

English: `Here begins this holy gospel, written by Saint [evangelist].`

Evidence: the boundary formula is as above. K&T give the following signs as
`write`, `holy/saint`, and the evangelist's name. At 054v01 the sequence ends
with K&T's own entry for Mark, and the codex's hand translation reads "Here
begins this holy gospel, written by Saint Mark."

## Belief and judgment

Gloss: `each,_every somebody be_saved`

English: `Everyone is saved` (when unqualified), or `Everyone who believes is
saved` when belief occurs in the same sentence.

Evidence: K&T give the first sign as `each, every`; their entry for the second
explicitly lists the combined expression at 019r03 as `every(body)`; the last
sign is `be saved`.

Gloss: `each,_every somebody be_damned`

English: `Everyone is damned` (when unqualified), or `Everyone who does not
believe is damned` when negated belief occurs in the same sentence.

Evidence: K&T give the same `every(body)` expression at 019r03 and give the
last sign as `be damned`. Context must supply the relative clause; the formula
must never become the contradictory "every man is saved, and one man is
damned."

Gloss sequence at 019r03–05 and its repetitions: `each,_every somebody ... one
somebody / be_damned ... Lord not believe ... one / be_saved but ... somebody
be_damned`

English: `Everyone who believes in the Lord is saved. No one who does not
believe is saved; whoever does not believe is damned.`

Evidence: K&T's `somebody` entry says the expression with `each/every` means
`every(body)` and, with negation at 019r03–04, `NOBODY`. Their numeral entry
gives `not one`, including 094r05, and their damnation entry quotes the sense
"not one sinner is saved, but all are damned" at 077r06. Their separate sign
at 019r04 is explicitly `not [believe]`; the conjunction at 019r04–05 has the
concessive senses `but, however` and a marked negative `not [be saved]`.

## Titles, acclamations, and common clauses

Gloss: `righteous(ly) son living God`

English: `the true Son of the living God`

Evidence: K&T give `righteous(ly)` the contextual senses `true, right, indeed`
and quote the full expression as "the true Son of the living God" at 005v08.
They give the next three signs as `son`, `living`, and `God`.

Gloss: `chapter-oh chapter-oh amen`

English: `O! O! Amen.`

Evidence: these are two repetitions of K&T's vocative particle `oh`, followed
by their `amen`; K&T cite 018r04 for `amen`. The preceding element is a sign
which elsewhere means `chapter`, but here the repeated compound is the
acclamation printed in the hand translation. Never print the apparatus phrase
"O chapter."

Gloss: `time say Lord-Jézus`

English: `At that time the Lord Jesus said ...`

Evidence: K&T give the signs as `time`, `say`, and `Lord Jesus`. Their entry for
the time sign gives the narrative sense `then` inside a story. The complement
and listener must still come from the folio.

Gloss: `from die stand_up`

English: `rose from the dead`

Evidence: K&T give the signs as `from`, `die/death`, and `stand up/rise`; the
same formula occurs in Resurrection passages including 059r and 181v.

Gloss: `and then-exist ...`

English: use `Then ...`, `There was ...`, or a finite verb demanded by the
clause; never print `then-exist` or merely replace it word for word.

Evidence: K&T give the compound's parts as narrative `then` and `exist/there
is`. The correct English depends on whether it introduces an event, a person,
or a state. Their time entry explicitly gives `then` as the sense used inside
a story.

Gloss: `and somebody exist ...`

English: use `There was a man ...`, `A man was ...`, `He ...`, or the personal
pronoun required by the surrounding lines; never print `somebody exists`.

Evidence: K&T give the signs as `and`, `somebody` (also `we`, `he/she`,
`everybody`, or `nobody` in cited expressions), and `exist/there is`. The
surrounding clause and K&T's cited example decide the person and syntax.

## Number rule

Gloss: any run of numeral signs

English: add the numerals; a `ten` after a group multiplies that group by ten.
Write the resulting number in words without dropping any group.

Evidence: K&T's numeral entries print the manuscript's compound examples,
including four tens as `40 days` at 004v11, six-six as `12 disciples` at
022r10, and the multi-part 5166 at 021r04–05. This is the standing arithmetic
rule in `CODEX_REWRITE.md`.
